# Tiling Search, 45° tilings: what we have and what is missing

The owner asked on 25 October 2026 whether the library covers the tilings on [Tiling Search's list of tilings whose angles are multiples of 45°](https://tilingsearch.mit.edu/sim/sim6.htm). The list has 161 tilings. Each was matched by eye against our 24 eight-fold tutorials and against the work queue and the Upcoming page.

**In short:** we have 4 of the 161. Three more are in the queue but parked. None of the eight-fold designs on the Upcoming page comes from this list. That leaves 154 missing: 20 from the Alhambra, the Generalife and other Nasrid work, 11 other Spanish ones, and 123 from Morocco, Iran, India and elsewhere. Our Patio de los Arrayanes squares (PG299) is a 45° tiling too, but this list leaves it out, so the list is not complete either.

“Size” is Wichmann’s count of polygons in his drawing, a rough guide to how hard a design is; most of our tutorials are under 250.

## What to build next

The queue’s next goal is more Alhambra panels paired with a photo, so these come first:

1. **AL2 and P051, the Mexuar corridor frets.** They are chiral T-shaped frets of the same family as our Sala de los Reyes fret (PG365), so its construction is the place to start.
2. **A233, Patio de los Leones.** Its eight-pointed stars ringed by bands look close to our Comares bath and Sala de la Barca stars, which share one construction.
3. **AL1 and F219, Salón de Comares**, both small (95 and 139 polygons).

These small ones from elsewhere would each make a quick beginner tutorial: F254L (Shah-i-Zinda, 55 polygons, octagons and four-pointed stars), W2 (Qaytbay floor, 127), P17 (Khatem Sulemani, 71), R9 (Topkapı Scroll, 65), CM006A and F34 (Roman mosaics).
