# Lessons from writing pattern tutorials

Each entry is a mistake that already cost time once, or a fact that saves time. Add to it whenever something surprises you.

## Research

- The Met Museum's educator PDF (`islamic_art_and_geometric_design.pdf`) answers scripts with an HTML page, not the PDF. Check every download with `file` before reading it.
- mamootdiy.com has numbered written steps with one picture per step for several patterns (six-fold model, four-fold model, five-fold model, six-fold hammerhead, Ibn Tulun window, six-fold snowflake, Alhambra rosette tile). Its pictures download with a plain `curl -A "Mozilla/5.0"`; drop the `?w=` suffix for full size.

## Format

- The cell may be a rectangle, a parallelogram whose lattice also repeats as a rectangle, or a centrally symmetric hexagon. Repeats exist for `rot3`, `rot4`, `rot5`, `rot6`, `rot8`, `rot10`, `rot12` and `rot16`.
- Use the hexagon around O as the cell for six- and twelve-fold patterns, not a rhombus. Free inking (and its test) only offers construction inside the cell, and a rhombus with a corner at O reaches into neighbouring hexagons where nothing was built.
- A step-by-step source often draws a star as lines, not as two triangles. Match the source's elements, not just its final shape, or the step comparison shows extra lines.
- Our renders are framed on the cell, so they can look smaller than the source. Crop the source to the same area before deciding a step does not match.
- Run `python3 tools/review/format_tutorial.py tutorials/<id>.json` after generating a tutorial with a script; `json.dump` with indent 2 spreads every element over a dozen lines.
- The golden test checks the tutorial's `fold` symmetry about O for pieces within distance 1 of O, so O must be a rosette centre and OA should be about the size of the rosette.

## Checking

- A source's suggested tile can be wrong even when its drawing is right. The ATM handout repeats the large hexagon of the star-and-hexagons pattern, which gives a different wall from the photo. Always compare the wall with a photo of the real pattern, not only with the source's drawing.
- Before building a tile, work out the expected piece areas by hand (star, hexagon, rhombus) and check that they add up to the cell area; when `pnpm review` prints the same numbers, the construction is right.

- When a source's final trace is intricate, sample its image along every construction segment between crossings and keep the ones drawn black (see the hammerhead log). Then overlay our ink on the source image to confirm; it is faster and surer than reading the drawing by eye.
- When a source's note and its written step disagree, measure the source drawing.
- One motif can tile several ways. Check which way the photo uses by the direction from one star to its neighbours, then pick the cell that gives that lattice.

- Before building, check that a source shows the pattern repeating, not just one motif. Standalone rosettes (like Mamoot's Alhambra rosette) cannot be verified as a wall.

- A pattern found through one tutorial needs its own photo. A similar-looking photo is not enough: compare the sizes of the stars and the pieces between them before assuming it is the same pattern.
- Patterns with dozens of lines are best generated by a script that derives the candidate lines from the construction, classifies the source drawing, and chooses pick rules by margin. See `docs/patterns/abandoned/gen-four-fold-ring-of-circles.py`; check line normals carefully (a line at 45° has its normal at 135°).

- Measure before deciding a mismatch. The dodecagram stars looked as if they overlapped in the source, but measuring showed they touch, exactly as ours do.
- Many good step-by-step sources (Mamoot, Tuts+, Sandy Kurt) never show their pattern on a real building. Find the photo first, before building, or the work may end abandoned.

- To read a periodic drawing, fold every line pixel into one symmetry wedge (translate to the nearest repeat centre, then turn and mirror into the wedge): the true lines pile up into sharp segments and noise spreads out (see the Córdoba research folder).
- Classify ink by face colours, not by distance to dark pixels, when the drawing is coloured: thick outlines make short false pieces look inked. But the faces must be cut by every line that reaches the tile, including lines of motifs centred in neighbouring tiles.
- Time-box a pattern: after about two hours without a clean pieces comparison, record the state in the queue and move on.

## Player

- CSS animations do not play inside an SVG `<pattern>`; animate an overlay instead (the colour veil in `Wall.tsx`).
- Dashes cannot draw a line with `vector-effect: non-scaling-stroke` along its length (the path length is ignored); fade strokes in instead.
- A CSS zoom on SVG content with `transform-box: view-box` does not centre on the view box. A SMIL `<animate>` on `viewBox` does, but its clock starts at mount, so after a slow first render it is out of step with Web Animations started in an effect; set `viewBox` from requestAnimationFrame on the same clock instead.
- Owner feedback on transitions (2026-10-04): they must look done by hand. Ink with a pen, line after line along each path, red while drawing and black once drawn; build the wall by copying the tile along into a row, then copying the row up (and across when shifted); paint one colour at a time.
- In jsdom, hundreds of elements each with an inline custom property are very slow; put the property on a parent group.
- Prettier rewriting a file while the dev server is reading it can leave Vite serving an empty module; `touch` the file to make it reload.
- To check an animation, drive headless Chrome over the DevTools protocol (`node` has `WebSocket`): press keys, take screenshots at chosen times, and read `element.getAnimations()` when a frame looks wrong.

## Coloring

- Same-shaped pieces often take different colors in real panels (Comares bones: white across, black and colored up, by column). `pattern.placements` colors by turn, by place in the cell and by cell of a larger repeat; check the photo's color repeat in cells before writing rules (the bones repeat every 2 × 2 cells).

## Tooling

- Never `git stash` (or check out other versions of files) while the owner's dev server runs: Vite picked up the old schema during a stash and kept serving it after the files came back, so two tutorials showed "Unrecognized key" until the server was restarted.

- Timed tests (the PRD speed targets) fail when they share the CPU with the golden suite; the engine runs them as their own Vitest group after everything else, and `pnpm test` runs projects one at a time.

- Agent worktrees live in `.claude/worktrees/` inside the repo. Without `.nxignore`, Nx picks them up as extra projects, the main test run fails with circular project references, and `pnpm typecheck` adds them to the root `tsconfig.json`. `.nxignore` now excludes `.claude/`.
- `perf.spec.ts` times a 3,000-segment arrangement against 300 ms. While several agents run tests at once it can take slightly longer; rerun it alone before treating it as a regression.
- Judge a reading by overlaying it in red on the source in black, never by putting two drawings side by side: side by side, a correct reading looked mirrored twice.
- Read grid drawings at half-square resolution: Alhambra grid designs often end lines at the centre of a square, which a whole-square reader misses.
- Read the source's captions for every picture before matching a tracing to a photo. The ALZADA article prints the Comares "huesos" photo next to two Torre de las Infantas tracings; I assumed the first tracing was of the photo and wrongly marked it verified. Overlay our lines on the photo itself before saying a design matches it.
- Nx cached test results across tutorial edits because `tutorials/` is outside every project, so `pnpm test` replayed old passes while several tutorials were broken (fixed: `sharedGlobals` in `nx.json` now includes `tutorials/**`). When in doubt, run `pnpm nx reset` before trusting a pass.
- Some real designs (the Infantas crosses, the Comares lattice) are pinwheels: four-fold rotation but no mirror. Mark them `"chiral": true`; the golden test then checks that the mirror really is absent.
- Free inking now offers the neighbouring tiles' construction inside the tile and keeps one copy of each edge piece, so slanted and hexagonal tiles work.

## The Bou Inania rosette, her way (5 October)

- When a drawing shows two parallel bands along each side of the tile, the real repeat may overlap the copies by one band: the two lines on the right of one copy are the two lines on the left of the next. Here the period is the big figure's side plus the frame (E + F), not twice the frame. Build the tile from one side's pair to the other side's inner line, and leave the lines on the right and top sides to the neighbouring copies, or they are drawn twice and leave slivers.
- At the corners where four overlapping copies meet, the content must be symmetric about the corner's own centre. The Bou Inania corner is an eight-pointed star: the little square between the two bands, plus a diamond whose side towards O is a line of set H. The diamond's sides carry on past its points to make the pointed ends of the long hexagons, and the square's sides are not inked between the star and those ends, so the same ten-cornered piece sits all round the star.
- Ink read from a picture leaves loose ends, bars across crossings and tiny triangles where two nearly equal lines both got inked. `tools/review/bou_inania_v2/9_clean_ink.py` fills short gaps along a line, drops runs of ink shorter than 0.05, drops what dangles, and opens tiny triangles; given the engine's dump it also opens any remaining piece under 0.001 in area.
- After every change to the ink, compare the whole list of piece shapes with the last version the owner approved. Piece names from `finish.py` are given by size and shift between builds, so compare shapes and counts, not names. A fix for the long hexagons once broke the corner star, and the owner found it first.
