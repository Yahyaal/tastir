# Six-fold hammerheads

**Sources.** Mamoot's "Six-Fold Hammerhead Pattern" gives seven written steps with a drawing for each, two ways to tile it, and photos of the pattern at the Alcázar of Seville and on a door of the Madrasa al-Zahiriye in Aleppo. No second step-by-step source was found; an earlier attempt's Tumblr download was a bot-check page.

**Steps.** The foundation, the three mirror lines and the two crossing hexagons matched the source's steps 1–3 on the first try. The hammerhead lines (each crossing joined to the one three places round) matched step 5 on the first try. For the inner star the source's note says its points reach as far as a hexagon corner is from the nearest crossing (0.268), but its written step (join where the hammerhead lines cross the small petal arcs) gives 0.25. Measuring the source drawing gave lines at 0.1265 from the centre, which is the written step's 0.125 and not the note's 0.134, so the tutorial follows the written step. The ink was found by sampling the source's final drawing along every construction segment, and an overlay shows every inked segment on the source's black lines.

**Decisions.** The motif fits both hexagons, and the source tiles it two ways. The first wall used the standing hexagon, which gives the source's right-hand tiling with a hexagon between every three motifs. The Alcázar photo has its stars on a lattice turned 30° from that, with diamonds and dark rectangles between, which is the source's left-hand tiling, so the tile is the hexagon lying on its side. A line drawing of our wall then matches the left-hand tiling line for line, long diagonals included.

**Result.** Five piece types: hammerheads (9 corners, six per tile), the small star, kites, triangles and rhombi. The line structure matches the source's tiling and the arrangement of the Alcázar panel (stars in columns, six hammerheads round each, diamonds between). The photo is strapwork, so its pieces are the bands' gaps rather than our pieces one for one.

Status: verified
