# Your own patterns: the build log

This is the working log for building the plan at `#/plan` (written in `apps/player/src/review/Plan.tsx`): learners make their own patterns, by saving their own inking and colours, by copying a tutorial at any step, from nothing, and from building blocks. The owner approved the plan and its order on 6 October 2026 and asked for it to be built by two agents, a builder and a tester who uses the app like a person and reports what goes wrong.

**If work stopped (a usage limit, a crash), resume here:** take the first unticked task below, read its notes and the latest tester report at the bottom, and carry on. Every finished task is ticked, committed with its number in the message, and pushed.

## The owner's decisions (6 October 2026)

The phases go in the order below. Every user pattern is a true ruler and compass construction: the editor only lets you click points that exist, and there is no free drawing. Patterns are kept in the browser first, then as a file and a share link; accounts come later, with a server. Good user patterns may join the library after review on the review page, with a golden test and the maker named in the credits. We use the editor ourselves to make library tutorials.

## How the two agents work

The builder takes one task at a time, shows nothing to the owner until it works, runs `pnpm test`, `pnpm lint` and `pnpm typecheck`, commits with the task number (“User patterns 1.2: …”), pushes, ticks the task here and adds anything learned to the lessons at the bottom. The builder never runs `pnpm format` over the whole repo (it rewrites the tool JSON in `tools/review/`); it runs Prettier on the files it changed.

The tester starts its own dev server on a spare port (never the owner's on 4200), drives the app in headless Chromium with `playwright-core` from `~/.cache/tastir-tester/` (the browser is `~/.cache/ms-playwright/chromium-1243/chrome-linux64/chrome`), and goes through each finished phase the way a learner would, from the home page, by clicking. It writes a dated report at the bottom of this file: what it tried, what worked, what was confusing or broken, with screenshots saved under `docs/review/user-patterns/` (ignored by git). The builder fixes what the report finds before moving on.

## Lessons

- An action that does more than the learner clicked must show it, or be off by default. One ink click took the nearest bit, ran it along a whole line and added its turned and mirrored copies, and the owner could not tell why several lines appeared; a pen that inks exactly from dot to dot, with copies as a choice that flashes as it draws, says what it does. (8 October)
- A grid item is as wide as its content unless it has `min-width: 0`: a stack of tool bars in one grid cell made the phone page 980 px wide, and a mobile browser then mapped taps wrongly. Check `document.documentElement.scrollWidth` in a mobile context with touch, not only a narrow desktop window. (7 October)
- When hints and a shortcut disagree, the learner gets stuck between them: a whole-line click on a ringed bit inked past what the pattern keeps. Make the shortcut follow the pattern (a run on a kept bit stops where the pattern's ink stops), and test by following the hints in a browser to the end. (7 October)
- To keep a layout still while its content changes, put every version of the content in one grid cell (`grid-area: 1 / 1`), show only the one at hand (`visibility: hidden`, `aria-hidden` and `inert` on the others), and the cell takes the size of the largest. Measuring heights in effects only covers the change you thought of: the wider Repeat and color it button narrowed the text and added a line. (7 October)
- A helper that rebuilds a table on every call is fine for one click and a hang inside a loop: `imagesOf` built a 100,000-entry map per call, and checking mirror symmetry over 547 bits froze the Mian rosette's ink. Cache such tables per object in a WeakMap. (7 October)
- In a grid laid out by order, a cell that is hidden (`hidden`, so `display: none`) shifts every later cell: the ink stage hid the hint cell and the board jumped to the top right. Keep every cell in both stages and change only what is inside it; a jsdom test now compares the grid's cells at a step and at the ink. (7 October)
- Mirror symmetry is a property of the pattern, not of the fold: check the tutorial's own ink (every inked bit's mirror image is inked too) before inking mirrored copies, or a pinwheel cannot be inked by hand. (7 October)
- A halo for SVG text (`paint-order: stroke`) must be sized in `em`: in a drawing whose units are OA, `stroke-width: 3px` is three units wide and painted gesso over the whole drawing. (7 October)
- Names a learner reads are the ones a tutorial's texts use or its steps highlight; every other point name (k.1, g6_90, J120) is for the engine and is never shown, on either half. (7 October)
- The editor's ink offers only bits a file can name, and on nine of the thirteen beginner patterns that is not enough to close the pieces (the ink runs along neighbouring tiles' lines). Drawing along therefore inks with the player's free-ink bits (`inkPieces` with `classicSelection` for Ink it for me), which reproduce all thirteen, and the kept pattern carries the tutorial's own ink taken onto the board by geometry (`withTutorialInk`), its extra points in one hidden step. (7 October)
- Draw it for me never copies the tutorial's names blindly: each reference is looked up on the board by geometry (a figure with `figureOn`, a point as a named place or a crossing with `pickFor`), and only what the board lacks is drawn, from the tutorial's spec with its references mapped. A turned copy of a point has no crossing to find, so its repeat is drawn instead. New points take the tutorial's name when it is free (E, c1), so the board reads like the step text. (7 October)
- Two drawings line up only when they have the same size and the same view: put the tool bar and the step's heading in one grid row above both, keep the captions under them at a fixed height, and give `useCamera` both drawings (a stable array of refs) so wheel and drag on either move one shared view. Never reuse a class name across components: the step strip's buttons were `.along-step`, the same as the step's plate, and squashed the drawing to 28 px. (7 October)
- Drawing along checks figures, never points, with a looser tolerance (`ALONG_EPS`, 1e-6) than the engine's 1e-9, because two routes to the same line round differently. A polygon counts when each side lies on a drawn line or along a drawn side, so a tile whose sides the earlier steps already ruled (the Alhambra grids) is done before its own step; that is right, not a bug, and the test allows it. (7 October)
- The review page routes by the address's `#`, so a plain `#card` link leaves the page for a tutorial of that name. Any link written for a card on the page must scroll instead, and a card that is not a tutorial must not link to the player. The owner hit both on 7 October. (7 October)

- `pnpm format` rewrites the JSON in `tools/review/` (thousands of lines of line breaks), which then looks like real changes; run Prettier only on the files you changed. (6 October)
- The player steps on Space and the arrow keys from a window listener, so any text box in the player must be skipped there, or typing a design's name steps the tutorial. (6 October)
- A design's wall needs its inked pattern, which is slow for big tutorials, so the worker makes it: `useModel(json, inked)` sends the pieces with the tutorial and the worker applies `withInking` (model.ts) before replying. The player itself still re-inks on the main thread, as free inking always did. (6 October)
- A pick rule must be checked against the crossings the engine sees, so `pickFor` (engine `picking.ts`) only proposes rules and then evaluates each with `crossingOf`, the same code a point step runs; `angle: 90` can pick a crossing at 45° when the other is at 225°, so the crossing's own round angle is tried first. (6 October)
- jsdom has no CSS, so two copies of a control hidden by media queries both show in tests and break `getByRole`; choose the one place by screen width in code (`useNarrow` in app.tsx) instead. (6 October)
- The engine stops at the first step it cannot evaluate, so the editor's `check` (editor/document.ts) sets that step aside with its reason and evaluates again until every step works or is marked broken; `evaluate` reads only the steps, so a draft with no ink or tile evaluates. Designs and patterns share one safe localStorage list (`app/storage.ts`). (6 October)
- Check every layout change at 1280, 1100, 1024, 900, 800 and 390 px: between 761 and 1100 px the player had a fixed screen height, so the colour panel, placed under the wall, was squeezed onto the wall (found by the tester, and older than phase 1). To compare with an old commit, use `git worktree add` in the scratch folder with symlinks to `node_modules` and `apps/player/node_modules`, and run its Vite on another port. (7 October)
- The editor snaps only near the click: it takes the elements within reach and crosses just those pairs, then asks `pickFor` for the rule, so it never lists every crossing of a big drawing. For jsdom tests, give the drawing a `getBoundingClientRect` and click at screen positions worked out from its `viewBox`. (7 October)
- The editor offers only the bits of line a file can name: each must run along a line or polygon side of the construction and end where an element crosses it. Bits drawn only by a neighbouring tile's copy are left out, because ink written outside the tile is repeated only one tile either way (computePattern) and would leave gaps. A user pattern plays at `#/mine/<id>`; the player gets an `address` so its designs and copies point back there. (7 October)
- Never leave half-done work in the main tree for long: the tester's dev server picked up unfinished editor code live and two polygon steps vanished in its run. The tester now tests from a frozen worktree of the last commit, which is the right way; the builder commits each task as soon as it passes. (7 October)
- Saving a pattern again keeps its piece names: `tutorialOf` computes the pieces with the draft's earlier expect block, so matching pieces keep their names, colours and placements, and only new ones get plain names. Every library tutorial round-trips through the editor and passes the golden checks (roundtrip.spec.ts, two seconds for all 34). (7 October)
- Never let a learner read an internal name (c, d1, S1): step texts and engine reasons go through `describe` and `plainReason` (editor/document.ts), which name lines, circles and shapes by how they were made; points keep their names, since the drawing labels them. (7 October)
- Symmetry mode needs nothing new in the format: each new element gets a `repeat` with `rotN` in its own step (the format has rot3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 10, 12 and 16; the editor offers 4, 6, 8, 12 and 16). Turned copies that land on each other (a line through O turned by 180°) do no harm: snapping skips lines lying along each other, and free inking merges equal bits. With it, the eight-fold star and cross is five clicked steps: circle, line ×4, square ×8, tangent ×4, tile. (7 October)
- When two tasks touch the same file, commit them one at a time from the start; splitting them afterwards meant copying files aside and briefly reverting my own uncommitted edits, which a live dev server would have shown. (7 October)
- Never run `git checkout -- <file>` to undo a formatter, even on your own files: it threw away a real edit to the format doc, which had to be written again. Undo by editing. (7 October)
- Phase 4 went in as one commit: the three tasks grew in the same two files (blocks.ts, Editor.tsx) and could not be split afterwards without reverting the working tree. Next time, commit 4.1's core before writing 4.2's blocks. (7 October)
- A block's internal names must be unique across all its steps (a circle `h1` and a corner `h1` clashed after renaming); blocks.spec.ts now checks every block for it. Blocks pick crossings by `nearest`/`farthest` a clicked point or by `points` (all crossings), never by angle, so they work on any circle, not only one about O. (7 October)

### Inking with a pen (6.11), tester report (8 October 2026)

I tested from a frozen worktree of b5fccb3, with my own server on port 4351, at 1280×800 and on a touch phone (390×844). I had not read how the pen is meant to work. Screenshots are in `docs/review/user-patterns/ink-pen-test/`. There were no console or page errors in any run.

**What I did.** I opened `#/alhambra-comares-bones/draw`, used Draw it for me through the six steps, and pressed Ink it. On the Ink stage I first tried to ink the bones by reading the screen and comparing my board with the picture on the right. Then I followed the hints alone, and finally tried copies, Erase, Undo, Redo and Escape. I did the same on the phone by tapping, then inked the star and cross.

**Is it now obvious what a click will do? Mostly yes.**
- The board says “Pen: Click a dot to put the pen down, then a dot on the same line to ink between them.”
- After the first click the pen shows as a saffron dot. Every dot it can reach turns blue, and a dashed line follows the pointer, so I could see the stroke before making it (`bones-rubber.png`).
- A second click on a blue dot inks exactly the straight stretch between the two dots, and the pen stays there to carry on (`bones-1280-b.png`).
- A dot that is not on a common line is refused with a clear message: “Those two points are not on one line; ink goes along the lines you drew.”
- “Lift the pen” and Escape lift it. Undo and Redo (and Ctrl+Z) take back or bring back one whole stroke. Erase works the same way, stroke by stroke.
- **One click never inked more than I expected while “Also ink the copies round the centre” was off, which is the default.** A stroke inks only the stretch between its two dots. A long stroke across several dots inks the whole straight stretch, which is what I asked for.
- With copies on, one stroke on the star and cross inked all sixteen copies at once, and the copies flashed saffron. That surprised me less, because the box says what it does.

**By hand, from the screen only.** The first strokes went as expected. Following the hints, both the bones and the star and cross reached “✓ Your ink closes the pattern's pieces”:
- the bones at 1280 in 10 strokes;
- the bones on the phone in 10 strokes, tapping;
- the star and cross in 2 strokes with copies on, with no hint.

At level 2 the hint rings the two dots of a stroke (“Put the pen on the first ringed dot, then click the second: a side of the bone”), and at level 3 every stroke still to ink is dashed. On the phone the page is 390 px wide and tapping dots works exactly as clicking does.

**Problems, most important first.** Bugs:

1. **A stroke along the tile's edge is inked on the opposite edge, not where I drew it.** On the bones, a pen stroke from (−1, 3) to (0, 4) runs along the top-left side of the tile, but the ink appears on the bottom-right side, at (3, −1)–(4, 0). The same happens for the other edges (`bones-tile-edge-crop.png`). Where I drew, nothing shows, and the ink that does appear is dark over the tile's saffron dashes and hard to see. This is exactly the owner's confusion: one click making ink appear where I did not expect it. Expected: the ink shows where it was drawn, even if the two edges are the same once repeated (show both, or flash the other one).
2. **The dots I must start from are not shown until the pointer is near them.** Before the pen is down, the board shows dots only on the two axes and at the tile corners (17 dots), not at the bone's own corners, such as (−1, 1) or (−1, 3). On a computer they appear when the pointer passes near; on a phone there is no pointer, so the screen says “Click a dot” while showing only dots that are mostly not where the bones turn (`bones-phone-ink-full.png`). Tapping an unshown crossing does work, but a learner would not know to try. Expected: show every crossing inside the tile as a small dot at the Ink stage, or at least on touch screens.

Suggestions:

3. On a phone the texts say “Click”; “Tap” would read better.
4. The level 2 hint says “the first ringed dot”, but the two rings look the same. Either order works, so “one ringed dot, then the other” would be clearer.
5. On the phone, the ink buttons (Undo, Redo, Clear the ink) sit at the end of a sideways-scrolling row and are cut off at the edge.

**Verdict: the owner's confusion is mostly fixed.** With the pen, a click no longer inks several lines in different directions. The dashed line shows the stroke before it is made, and with the default settings a stroke inks exactly the stretch between two dots. Undo, Erase and the hints all work by stroke, on a computer and on a phone. Two things remain before I would call it obvious: strokes along the tile's edge must not move to the opposite edge (1), and the dots a learner needs should be visible before the pen is down, especially on a phone (2).
