Miniflare runs the actual Worker and SQLite-backed Durable Object. Checks cover project/origin/purpose binding, forged and expired signatures, unauthorized page publication, room isolation and fanout, duplicate/out-of-order/concurrent revision hints, runtime auto-response ping/pong, and no data after a lease expires. Actual `unsafeEvictDurableObject(..., { webSockets: 'hibernate' })` preserves the connected socket; publication wakes the room and delivers the next revision with its last revision retained. Expiry checks compare an expired socket with a fresh peer; they do not depend on the timing of a completed transport close handshake.

The browser client tests cover failed or hanging ticket requests, failed or hanging authoritative reads, serialized/coalesced reads, catching up to a hint, duplicate messages, periodic reconciliation, heartbeat timeouts, ticket renewal, reconnects, polling and cancellation/cleanup.

## Local browser/Worker fixture

One Chromium tab used the real public client against Wrangler on `127.0.0.1:8797`, with a temporary fixture origin `127.0.0.1:8798`. The fixture's ticket endpoint and authoritative project reads are simulated; the browser WebSocket, signed server publication, Worker and Durable Object are real.

The observed signed hint-to-fixture-render time was **36 ms**; an update after switching the client to an unreachable room appeared through fallback reads in **1189 ms**. The separate signed HTTP publish round trip was **30 ms**. These are single local observations, not deployed Studio measurements. After cleanup, no further project reads occurred during a 3.3-second observation. The temporary fixture/browser and Wrangler processes were stopped afterward. The generated `.dev.vars` remains ignored and owner-readable only; its secret was not printed.
