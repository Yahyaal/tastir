# Tastir redesign review — 14 October 2026

I prefer this redesign. The warm paper, restrained blue actions, serif headings, and cedar drawing frame suit the craft. The changes improve the lesson flow as well as its appearance. I would keep this direction and make a focused usability pass.

## What improved

The starter button now opens Watch at step 1. Lesson text sits beside a large drawing on desktop, and the four-stage timeline makes the sequence easier to understand. Watch resumes the previous step and the home page offers Continue. Draw-along uses the owner-approved single board with a faint guide, and the current step’s tools appear before More tools. Paint colors and Undo now precede the palettes, ring buttons make coloring more direct, and the phone’s paint swatches are 44 × 44 px. The light and dark themes worked, including persistence after reload. The new “Kept in My patterns” confirmation makes completion clearer.

## What I would fix next

### 1. Tablet results expand and open at the bottom — fix first

At 1024 × 768, opening the star-and-cross result scrolled the page to y = 1,447. The drawing became 2,027 px high beside the full color panel, making stars enormous and leaving the header above the viewport. I reproduced the same behavior at 820 × 1180. Watch and Draw along do fit these sizes.

**Change.** Keep the drawing height bounded at tablet widths, with the color panel in its own scrolling area or a deliberate stacked layout. Reveal the current timeline item by scrolling its horizontal container rather than the whole document.

**Check.** Opening Result at 1024 and 820 px widths starts at the artwork and color controls; changing stages preserves a sensible drawing size.

**Location.** `apps/player/src/styles.css:3146; apps/player/src/app/Player.tsx:1363`

### 2. First-time color changes are still easy to lose — fix first

I chose Marrakech on a new result, followed All patterns, and reopened the pattern. There was no leave prompt and the palette returned to Marinid zellige. Named designs have save protection; the guard still excludes an unnamed first design.

**Change.** Keep a recoverable browser draft from the first ink, color, or style edit. Show the saved state consistently. If a draft cannot be kept, protect the first changes when leaving and make a backup action available.

**Check.** An unnamed edit survives reload or navigation, or the learner receives a clear chance to preserve it.

**Location.** `apps/player/src/app/Player.tsx:302`

### 3. The phone board arrives before the actionable prompt — fix first

On a 390 × 844 phone, the board starts around y = 168, tools at y = 615, and step text around y = 698. The fixed bottom buttons begin near y = 772 and initially cover part of the first instruction. “Choose Compass, tap O, then E” is further below the first viewport. The Compass outline is a suggestion; aria-pressed is false until it is selected.

**Change.** Keep the approved single board and faint guide. Put one short live prompt beside the tools or at the top edge of the board: “Tap the centre O”, then “Tap E”. Preselect the suggested tool in a guided lesson, or distinguish its hint outline from a selected state. Let detailed explanation remain below. Make “Guide: dashed; your drawing: solid” explicit.

**Check.** A first-time phone user knows what to tap while both target points are visible, and can tell whether a tool is active.

**Location.** `apps/player/src/along/DrawAlong.tsx:546; apps/player/src/along/DrawAlong.tsx:1782; apps/player/src/along/along.css:840`

### 4. Save and completion need a prominent home — next

The color tools now appear first, which is better. Save as my design still sits around y = 1,427 in the desktop scrolling column and y = 2,099 on a phone. The phone keeps Replay, Back, and a disabled Next fixed at the bottom of the finished result.

**Change.** At the final stage, use the stable bottom or header action area for Save and Share, with a clear “Your pattern is ready” message. Move related patterns and Make a variation after these actions. Keep Back available as a secondary lesson action.

**Check.** A learner recognizes completion and finds Save without scrolling past palettes, recommendations, and output controls.

**Location.** `apps/player/src/app/Player.tsx:1036; apps/player/src/app/Player.tsx:1077`

### 5. Painting should keep the result visible — next

Phone paint swatches are now 44 × 44 px and appear around y = 550, much earlier than before. Ring buttons offer a convenient alternative to tapping small pieces. But the wall scrolls away while choosing lower rings or palettes, especially on a detailed Bou Inania pattern. Rosette A and B are not identified on the wall.

**Change.** Keep a compact artwork preview visible when working further down the color panel, or offer a larger editable repeating unit. Highlight the affected rosette and ring when a ring button is focused or hovered. Preserve the direct swatches and one-click ring painting; avoid another mandatory dialog.

**Check.** Users can see which part changed while choosing rings or palettes, and can identify Rosette A versus B.

**Location.** `apps/player/src/app/ColourPanel.tsx:283; apps/player/src/app/Wall.tsx`

### 6. The guided toolbar still has small touch controls — next

Color swatches and many main actions are larger now. Compass, More tools, Symmetry, Undo, and Redo remain about 26 px high on the phone; hint actions are about 31 px high. Swatches still announce hexadecimal values. Arrow keys now navigate drawing steps, but choosing board points is still a pointer interaction.

**Change.** Enlarge frequent drawing and hint controls for touch, with enough space to avoid accidental taps. Give colors useful names alongside their hex values. Preserve arrow-key step navigation and continue toward keyboard selection of existing points and lines.

**Check.** Touch drawing does not require precise taps on tiny toolbar controls, and users can distinguish colors and select geometry with suitable input methods.

**Location.** `apps/player/src/editor/ToolBar.tsx:55; apps/player/src/app/ColourPanel.tsx:257; apps/player/src/editor/EditorDrawing.tsx`

### 7. Free-editor inking still changes more than one segment by default — next

The free editor still starts with Keep it symmetric and Whole lines between points enabled. Draw-along leaves copied strokes off initially. The new look makes tools easier to read, but the effect of an editor ink click can still surprise someone moving from a guided lesson.

**Change.** Use a single-segment starting state and preview any added whole-line or symmetric scope. If automatic copies stay enabled, make the full affected set visible before the click and keep Undo close.

**Check.** The user can predict which lines an ink click will change in either mode.

**Location.** `apps/player/src/editor/Editor.tsx:305`

### 8. Continue from home covers watching, but not drawing practice — later

The new Continue card correctly returned to Watch step 4 after reload. Draw-along separately restored the board and current stage. The home card reads the watch-lesson store, so an unfinished drawing session is not surfaced through the same entry.

**Change.** Include unfinished draw-along sessions in Continue, identify the mode, and reopen the saved board. Keep the existing watch resume and explicit restart option.

**Check.** A learner who closes a drawing lesson can resume it directly from home without first remembering its pattern.

**Location.** `apps/player/src/app/Library.tsx:439; apps/player/src/app/lessons.ts; apps/player/src/along/progress.ts`

### 9. The library is calmer, but still needs search and filter recovery — later

Cards now have a clear main lesson action, with Result and Draw along as quieter alternatives. Filters use fewer visible controls. There is still no search box or Clear all action; an empty result still says “both choices”, even though there are three filters.

**Change.** Add simple search by name or place and a Clear all control beside active filters. Describe empty results using the selected filters. Keep the current simpler cards and remembered scroll position.

**Check.** A returning learner finds a named pattern quickly and recovers from an empty filter combination with one clear action.

**Location.** `apps/player/src/app/Library.tsx:346`

## My assessment of the visual design

The light theme is my preference for learning: it resembles a drawing surface, gives the pattern room, and makes the interface quieter. The dark theme is a useful alternative and the drawing roles remain recognizable in both. The repeating star motif in the timeline fits the subject. The frame is strong enough already; I would spend the next effort on clear prompts and saved-work actions rather than adding more decoration.

For the single-board lesson, keep the faint guide and the learner’s own lines visibly different. The board is a better use of space than the previous side-by-side construction view, and it avoids making the learner constantly compare two pictures. The remaining work is explaining which marks are a guide, which belong to the learner, and what their next click should do.

## Browser checks and limits

I reviewed commit `2c46470` plus the existing local review content. I inspected light and dark themes at desktop 1280 × 800, tablet 1024 × 768, and phone 390 × 844. I also checked the main layouts at 820 × 1180 and 320 × 740. All 39 library result routes reached Color without a page error. The measured layouts had no document horizontal overflow; that does not establish that their placement is correct, as the tablet regression demonstrates.

I reproduced the starter action, watch reload and Continue behavior, and loss of an unnamed palette edit. Ring painting changed the piece preview and Undo restored it. A named design saved and opened from a share link in a fresh context. I manually drew the first circle, then used drawing assistance for the rest of the beginner construction, inked it, repeated it, reached Color, and kept it. The drawing restored on reload. The editor’s light/dark theme and remembered choice were checked after transitions settled. These are browser observations and design judgments, not findings from a novice-user study or a historical-pattern verification.

The existing browser regressions also need a review alongside the redesign. For example, `apps/player/e2e/browsing.spec.ts` still expects clicking a library card and moving between patterns to open Result; cards now intentionally open Watch. Those expectations should follow the new intended behavior. Add browser coverage for the tablet drawing height and initial scroll position, first-design recovery, and a phone drawing action with the prompt visible.
