# Tastir usability review — 13 October 2026

Tastir’s biggest opportunity is clearer guidance through a task. The geometry and drawing presentation are strong; the first lesson, editing tools, and coloring controls currently ask users to make too many decisions before they understand the next action.

## What I reviewed

This is a review of the current `main` checkout in Chromium, using the local dev server. I read the user-facing routing, library, player, draw-along, editor, storage, sharing, coloring, style, original-photo, and print components. I opened all 39 library results; every one reached Color without a page error. I inspected desktop (1280 × 800), tablet (1024 × 768), and phone (390 × 844) layouts, and walked through a beginner draw-along lesson with the provided assistance, then inked, repeated, colored, and kept it. I also checked manual touch drawing of the first circle, free-editor creation and variation, progress recovery, saving, sharing in a fresh browser context, SVG/PNG downloads, print-sheet rendering, original-photo dialog recovery, and invalid-file feedback.

These are observed behaviors and design recommendations, not findings from a study with real novice users. Catalog checks establish that results load; they do not verify every tutorial’s historical accuracy or every construction instruction. Local load times are not a performance benchmark. The dev-only navigation and private review material were not treated as public product features. Planned features and review mocks were not counted as current functionality.

## What already works well

The paired draw-along boards have matching dimensions and a shared camera. The orange/blue geometry distinction, progressive hints, and assisted drawing are useful foundations. Draw-along restores the board and current step; the free editor saves automatically. Library filters and the return to a previous card are preserved. Saving, share-link opening in a fresh context, keeping a shared copy, SVG and PNG downloads, and the printable line sheet worked. Original-photo inspection closed with Escape and returned focus to its opener. Invalid pattern-file feedback was understandable. Preserve these behaviors during any redesign.

## The first changes I would make

1. Make the home-page starter action open a guided first step.
2. Recover a first design’s changes before it has been named and saved.
3. Keep the mobile drawing, active tool or color, and Undo visible together.
4. Guide the next click and introduce advanced drawing tools gradually.
5. Preview inking scope and start automatic whole-line/symmetry helpers off.
6. Enlarge frequent touch controls and provide keyboard geometry selection.

## A clearer journey

Home: Continue a lesson, start the first lesson, or browse patterns. A lesson starts with a finished preview and a clear goal. Each drawing step shows its current tool and next target. After Draw, Ink, Repeat, and Color, a completion view offers Save, Share, and another lesson. Advanced tools remain available when the learner wants them.

## Findings, in priority order

### 01. Make the starter button begin a lesson (first)

The home page says “Start with the star and cross”, but the click opens the finished Color stage. A newcomer lands among twelve palettes before seeing a construction step. I reproduced that exact click.

**Suggested change.** Use “Start your first lesson” as the primary action and open a guided first step. Offer “Watch first” beside it. Keep finished previews available when browsing cards, and say what each card action opens.

**Done when.** A first-time visitor reaches step 1 from the main action, understands the first click, and can reach a finished preview through a clearly named secondary action.

**Where.** `apps/player/src/app/Library.tsx:237; apps/player/src/app/app.tsx:218`

### 02. Protect a design before its first named save (first)

I chose Marrakech on an unsaved result, followed All patterns, and reopened the result. The app returned to Marinid zellige without asking about the change. The leave guard currently applies only when a named design already exists.

**Suggested change.** Keep a recoverable local draft as soon as ink, color, or style changes. Show a quiet “Draft saved in this browser” status and let users name a version when they want. Include first-time changes in leave protection if recovery is unavailable.

**Done when.** A color change made before the first named save survives reload and leaving the page. A storage failure remains visible and offers a file backup.

**Where.** `apps/player/src/app/Player.tsx:289; apps/player/src/app/SaveDesign.tsx:48`

### 03. Keep the drawing and its working controls together (first)

At 390 × 844, the Color panel begins about 922 px down the page and the paint swatches about 1,392 px down. The wall is near the top. On the 1024 × 768 tablet, the panel starts about 866 px down. Users must move between the artwork and its controls.

**Suggested change.** Use a compact color tray beside or below a retained drawing area. Keep the active color, Undo, and the current task in view. Let more palettes and style details expand, with directly visible swatches. In Draw along, put the tool prompt above the board; it currently appears below it.

**Done when.** On phone and tablet, a user can choose a color, paint a piece, see the result, and undo while the artwork stays visible. The two draw-along boards retain their equal size and shared camera.

**Where.** `apps/player/src/app/Player.tsx:1023; apps/player/src/styles.css; apps/player/src/along/along.css`

### 04. Teach one tool and one click at a time (first)

The first draw-along step exposes eleven tools plus symmetry. The short prompt “Choose Compass, click O, then E” sits below the boards; on the phone it is below the first viewport. A highlighted hint can look like a selected tool even though choosing Compass is still required.

**Suggested change.** For guided lessons, preselect the relevant tool and show “Tap the centre O” followed by “Tap E to set the opening” directly above the drawing. Make the next target visually obvious. Put advanced tools behind one optional expansion; keep all tools available in the free editor. Preserve the existing hint and Draw it for me actions.

**Done when.** A new learner draws the first circle without scrolling to find the prompt or learning the whole toolbar. Tool selection and a hint highlight are visibly distinct.

**Where.** `apps/player/src/editor/ToolBar.tsx:42; apps/player/src/along/DrawAlong.tsx:534`

### 05. Make the scope of inking visible before a click (first)

In the free editor, Keep it symmetric and Whole lines between points are both on initially. One click can ink a whole run and copies around the centre. Draw-along already leaves copied strokes off initially, so the two experiences differ.

**Suggested change.** Start free inking with a single segment. Keep symmetry and whole-line helpers directly available, and preview every segment that the next click will affect. When helpers are enabled, state the effect beside the board. Use the same default across the editor and guided drawing.

**Done when.** The learner can predict every segment a click will change. One Undo restores all changes made by that click.

**Where.** `apps/player/src/editor/Editor.tsx:304; apps/player/src/editor/Editor.tsx:324; apps/player/src/along/useInkBoard.ts:56`

### 06. Show what a paint click will recolor (next)

Ring by ring paints immediately with the current brush; By shape opens a piece chooser. Switching methods also resets custom choices, although Undo can recover the switch. “Double the unit” and “Halve the unit” require knowledge of how rosettes repeat.

**Suggested change.** Give painting a consistent “choose a color, tap a target” behavior. Highlight the affected ring or matching shapes, and label the scope in plain language. Offer a large editable repeating unit alongside the wall, with visual examples for one rosette, alternating rosettes, and four rosettes. Preserve color choices per method where possible and keep Undo visible.

**Done when.** After painting, users can identify which pieces changed and reverse the action. Changing the coloring method explains the effect on existing choices.

**Where.** `apps/player/src/app/ColourPanel.tsx:119; apps/player/src/app/ColourPanel.tsx:206; apps/player/src/app/Player.tsx:651`

### 07. Bring the most useful controls to the top (next)

Twelve palette presets occupy roughly 318 px of the desktop panel. Save as my design begins around y = 1,038, below the panel viewport; Make a variation precedes it. Style and export controls require further panel scrolling. The current drawing and panel remain usable, but important actions are easy to miss.

**Suggested change.** Show a small set of suggested palettes and directly visible paint swatches first, with the full collection available on demand. Keep Save and Share in a stable action area. Put artwork styling beside color controls, and move related patterns and variations after the finished result.

**Done when.** A user can find Save, Share, the active color, and Undo without hunting through the side panel. Palette names and colors remain directly readable.

**Where.** `apps/player/src/app/Player.tsx:1023; apps/player/src/app/ColourPanel.tsx:83`

### 08. Use clear names for view, lesson stage, and completion (next)

The player offers Patterns, All patterns, Previous/Next pattern, Watch it drawn/Result/Draw along, a list of step numbers, Ink/Repeat/Color, and another Back/Next pair. At the final Color stage, the primary Next button is disabled. On phones, Ink/Repeat/Color lose their visible labels and show icons.

**Suggested change.** Keep the current pattern and mode clear in the header. Use Draw → Ink → Repeat → Color as a compact lesson path, with individual step numbers available when needed. Name pattern navigation explicitly. At completion, show “Your pattern is ready” with Save, Share, and a next lesson instead of a disabled Next.

**Done when.** Users can distinguish changing a pattern from moving one construction step, and recognize the end of the lesson. Stage names remain visible on phones.

**Where.** `apps/player/src/app/PatternNav.tsx:17; apps/player/src/app/Player.tsx:1254; apps/player/src/app/ViewSwitch.tsx:9`

### 09. Make finding a suitable pattern easier (next)

The library has 39 patterns, three actions per card, and Tradition, Symmetry, and Level filters. There is no search box. An empty combination says “both choices” and suggests tradition or level, even though symmetry is a third filter; I reproduced this with 16-fold and Beginner.

**Suggested change.** Add search by pattern name, place, and common shape terms. Put a small “Good first lessons” collection near the top, with difficulty, step count, and a measured time estimate. Show active filters together with Clear all. Explain an empty result using the actual selected filters. Retain the saved filters and return-to-card behavior.

**Done when.** A beginner finds a suitable lesson quickly, and a returning user finds a named pattern without scanning all cards. Empty results always offer a one-click recovery.

**Where.** `apps/player/src/app/Library.tsx:268; apps/player/src/app/Library.tsx:351`

### 10. Explain star points and repeat symmetry separately (next)

Titles such as “Eight-fold star and cross” and the twelve-point Bou Inania rosette appear alongside metadata or related-pattern groups marked 4-fold. These may describe different aspects of the geometry, but the interface does not explain the distinction. Terms such as rosette, grout, tile, and unit also arrive before an introduction.

**Suggested change.** Label the visual star by its number of points and the repeating layout by its symmetry, with a tiny diagram explaining the difference. Introduce a tile as “the part we repeat” and a rosette as “a star with rings of pieces around it”. Use the same short terms in tools, lessons, and color controls.

**Done when.** A learner can explain why a twelve-point star may belong to a fourfold repeating layout. The labels agree throughout the lesson.

**Where.** `apps/player/src/app/Library.tsx:513; apps/player/src/app/ExploreNext.tsx:75; tutorials/bou-inania-rosette.json`

### 11. Make resumed work visible on the home page (next)

Draw-along preserves the board and step, and shows Continue drawing on return. The free editor also saves changes automatically. The home page lists named designs and patterns, but does not surface unfinished draw-along sessions. Watch mode went from step 4 back to step 1 after reload.

**Suggested change.** Add “Continue your lesson: step 4 of 6” above the library for an unfinished lesson. Keep a short explanation of browser-local storage nearby. Remember a paused watch step and offer Resume or Start again; an explicit Watch from beginning action can still replay the full lesson.

**Done when.** A returning learner resumes an unfinished lesson from home without remembering the pattern name. A paused watch lesson has an explicit restart choice.

**Where.** `apps/player/src/along/DrawAlong.tsx:239; apps/player/src/along/DrawAlong.tsx:358; apps/player/src/app/Player.tsx:207`

### 12. Give drawings and color versions a shared home (next)

The app uses My patterns for editable constructions and My designs for ink/color/style versions. The editor says “Kept in this browser”, while the player asks for a manual named save and draw-along uses “Keep it”. Portable pattern-file download sits in the editor’s About panel; the player’s export choices are SVG and PNG.

**Suggested change.** Use a visible My work entry even before the first save. Give each item a useful description such as “Drawing in progress” or “Color version of Star and cross”. Use consistent saved/draft status and one Save a copy action. Put a portable file backup alongside image downloads, with “Editable pattern file”, “PNG image”, and “SVG vector” explanations.

**Done when.** Users understand what is kept, where it lives, and which download can be edited again in another browser. They can reach their work from any main screen.

**Where.** `apps/player/src/app/Shelf.tsx:43; apps/player/src/editor/PatternShelf.tsx:48; apps/player/src/editor/AboutPanel.tsx:319`

### 13. Make the free editor’s path to a finished pattern visible (later)

A blank pattern opens all drawing tools, an empty step panel, and Play text explaining that a tile is needed. The next tasks—construct, choose a tile, ink, and check the repeat—are spread across modes and panels. Building blocks exist, but a newcomer must discover them.

**Suggested change.** Offer Blank drawing and a few visual starting blocks when creating a pattern. Show a small completion checklist: draw the construction, choose the repeating tile, ink closed pieces, preview the repeat. Use each item to jump to the relevant control. Keep the technical tutorial metadata in About for users who need it.

**Done when.** A user can take a starter construction through to a playable pattern without searching several panels for the missing requirement.

**Where.** `apps/player/src/editor/Editor.tsx:447; apps/player/src/editor/BlocksPanel.tsx; apps/player/src/editor/AboutPanel.tsx`

### 14. Make dependency-breaking edits easy to recover (later)

The step-delete dialog explains which later steps rely on the selected step, then offers “Delete all” or “Only step”. The latter deliberately leaves broken steps. Deleting a pattern can likewise leave its saved designs unable to open. These are useful advanced choices, but beginners need a clearer consequence and recovery path.

**Suggested change.** Preview affected steps or designs before deletion. Prefer archive or reversible deletion for saved work, with a visible Undo action. If a user chooses to leave dependent steps, say “Delete this step and repair the affected steps” and guide them to the first broken one.

**Done when.** A deletion explains its effect in plain language and offers a clear way to recover. Existing detailed engine errors remain available for repair.

**Where.** `apps/player/src/editor/StepPanel.tsx:113; apps/player/src/editor/PatternShelf.tsx:247`

### 15. Increase touch targets and make colors and geometry accessible (first)

Step buttons and paint swatches measure 28 × 28 px; several tool buttons are about 22–30 px high. Ring-color swatches announce hexadecimal codes instead of color names. The main editable geometry surfaces handle pointer clicks but do not expose a keyboard point-selection flow. Small point names use #64748b on #091529, approximately 3.83:1 contrast.

**Suggested change.** Use roughly 44 × 44 px touch targets where practical, friendly color names plus the optional hex value, and larger point labels. Add keyboard selection of existing points and lines, with a clear current target. Preserve existing focus outlines, reduced-motion support, and modal focus handling. Test active labels and essential geometry for contrast; an optional light drawing surface could help some learners.

**Done when.** The first drawing and a coloring action can be completed using a keyboard. Phone controls are easy to hit. Active text meets appropriate contrast requirements, and geometry states remain understandable beyond color alone.

**Where.** `apps/player/src/editor/EditorDrawing.tsx:428; apps/player/src/app/ColourPanel.tsx:227; apps/player/src/editor/editor.css:261`

### 16. Keep coloring behavior consistent across modes (later)

The result player offers palette strips, a brush, and ring-by-ring coloring. Draw-along’s Color stage instead uses a Palette dropdown and a shape chooser. A learner who finishes drawing must learn a different coloring interaction from the one shown in Result.

**Suggested change.** Use the same palette previews, swatches, names, and Undo controls in both modes. If the learner’s hand-drawn wall only supports shape coloring, explain that scope directly. Preserve the drawing practice layout and show a clear path to customize the finished result further.

**Done when.** Finishing a drawn lesson teaches a coloring action that behaves predictably when the learner opens the result player.

**Where.** `apps/player/src/along/DrawAlong.tsx:979; apps/player/src/app/ColourPanel.tsx:83; apps/player/src/app/PiecePanel.tsx:82`

### 17. Connect construction steps to a visible learning goal (later)

The app explains what to draw and gives hints, but the first experience does not set a small achievement or explain the key geometric idea in each lesson. The long roadmap uses specialist terms and is prominent in the main library navigation.

**Suggested change.** Before a lesson, show the finished pattern, what the learner will learn, and the few tools required. Add a brief “Why this works” explanation at key moments, grounded in the existing construction. Keep sources and original photos close to the lesson. Present the roadmap as a secondary exploration page, with short summaries and optional deeper instructions.

**Done when.** A beginner knows what they are learning and sees how their current construction relates to the finished design. Research previews are clearly distinguished from playable lessons.

**Where.** `apps/player/src/app/Library.tsx:163; apps/player/src/app/UpcomingPatterns.tsx; apps/player/src/app/Original.tsx`

### 18. Make style and export choices visual and understandable (later)

Tiles, Strapwork, Interlace, Lines, and Jali are in a Style dropdown. Jali then provides directly visible material and finish choices, which are easier to inspect. The SVG/PNG, share-link, and print paths worked in browser checks, but their placement and technical labels make them less discoverable.

**Suggested change.** Offer small previews for the five styles and a one-line explanation of Jali and Interlace. Follow the direct-choice approach already used by Jali materials. Group output under Share, Download image, Download editable file, and Print, with format details available when useful. Show what area or repeat the export will contain.

**Done when.** Users can select a style by its appearance and choose an output that matches their goal without knowing file-format jargon.

**Where.** `apps/player/src/app/Player.tsx:1060; apps/player/src/app/Player.tsx:1119; apps/player/src/app/JaliControls.tsx:98`

## Implementation order

**Pass 1: a successful first lesson.** Address findings 01–05 and the frequent touch controls in 15. Keep all existing board dimensions and camera synchronization. The acceptance test is a new learner reaching the first circle from home, knowing the next click, and retaining any changes after leaving.

**Pass 2: predictable coloring and return visits.** Address 06–12 and 16. Keep paint swatches, Undo, Save, and Share near the artwork; add a visible Continue lesson entry. Test the same choose–paint–undo task on desktop, tablet, and phone.

**Pass 3: confident independent creation.** Address 13–14 and 17–18, and finish keyboard drawing. Use starter blocks, a completion checklist, dependency recovery, and visual style choices to help learners create their own pattern.

## How to validate the improvements

Ask five people who have not used Tastir to start the recommended lesson, draw the first circle, finish the pattern, recolor one ring or shape, save it, and reopen it. Treat four of five completing without coaching as an initial goal, then tune from observed failures. Record time to the first correct action, requests for help, missed tools, repeated scrolling, and successful recovery. Separately test keyboard-only drawing, touch on a physical phone, text enlargement, reduced motion, and storage failure.

Keep browser regression coverage for equal drawing boards, stable layout between stages, first-design recovery, correct starter routing, painting scope, save/share round trips, and a mobile paint action with artwork visible. The current successful load check of all 39 patterns is a useful baseline.
